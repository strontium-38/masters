\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

A direct comparison of the co-digestion and \gls{cgl} only phases reveals that the co-digestion of \gls{fw} and \gls{cgl} resulted in higher biogas yields and more stable operation. The pH was decreasing faster with the co-digestion, where with \gls{cgl} only phase it wasn't decreasing that fast, and was even growing with the \gls{khco3} that was added to maintain the pH. These results support the hypothesis that \gls{cgl}, when co-digested with \gls{fw}, can enhance biogas production by balancing the \gls{cn_ratio} and providing readily available carbon, whereas \gls{cgl} alone may work, but would need a different setup and ajustments.

The \gls{cgl} only phase, can't be said to be strictly worse than the co-digestion of \gls{fw} and \gls{cgl}, since both phases were done in sequence in the same reactor, this could have given the \gls{cgl} pahse disavantages on a contaminated enviroment.

Using \gls{cgl}, with some \gls{pgl} to make the necessary ajustment to balance the \gls{cn_ratio}, could be a viable solution for the \gls{cgl} problem. It's only major problem, is that \gls{cgl} contains a lot of inpurities that can affect the anaerobic digestion, and for a system that can reliably consume it, it will need a better control of it's substrate.

During the whole process, the biggest production of \gls{ch4} was when \gls{vfa_alk} was \approx\num{1} with a pH of \approx\num{6.5}, during the \gls{cgl} only phase, even with not optmal valuos of pH and \gls{vfa_alk}, it was able to maintain a resonable amount of production of \gls{ch4}, showing that it can be a suitable substrate for \gls{ad}.

Future studies could involve on how to use better the \gls{cgl} as a feed, to try subside it's downside of it's impurities and need for an extra caron rich substrate to balance the feed.
